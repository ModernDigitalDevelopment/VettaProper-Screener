# 2022 data — what arrived, what is usable

## The options data could not be used

Four archives were uploaded (3.3 GB) from Polygon flat files. The options
archives were **trade** data, not **quote** data. The 2023 ThetaData was quote
data. They are not interchangeable.

Columns present:
```
ticker, volume, open, close, high, low, window_start,
transactions, underlying, expiration, option_type, strike
```

Columns the engine requires but which are **absent**: `bid`, `ask`, `delta`.

### Why they could not be synthesised

**Bid/ask is the decisive variable.** Fill quality is worth 41% of the result
(§15), and a 30% execution shortfall turns +224% into −27% (§13). Inventing a
spread model means the answer becomes a function of that assumption rather than
of 2022.

**Delta drives strike selection**, and it would have to be derived from trade
prices. Measured sparsity on a sample day (2022-01-03, 103,475 contracts):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Contracts trading 1–2 times all day | **50%** |
| Median minute-bars per contract | **3** |
| Contracts with any trade in the final 30 min | **29%** |

71% of contracts had no late-session print to sample a closing price from.
Backing IV out of a single mid-morning trade on an illiquid strike, then
computing delta from it, is not a basis for strike selection.

The options archives were deleted rather than processed. 2022 options data will
be re-pulled from ThetaData in the quote schema the engine already reads.

## The stock data is complete and was kept

`stocks_minute_filtered.zip` — 251 trading days, full calendar year 2022,
491 symbols, minute OHLCV. No gaps.

### Processing

Minute bars collapsed to daily, **regular session only (09:30–16:00 ET)**,
with 2022 DST handled (Mar 13 – Nov 6). Polygon minute files include pre/post
market bars; including them would distort open, high, low and volume relative
to how the 2023 data was built.

Result: **123,486 daily bars**, then indicators on each symbol's series:
**98,793 rows** across 493 symbols (1 skipped for under 60 bars).

Artifacts, all committed:

| File | Size | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `daily_2022.csv.gz` | 2.2 MB | symbol, date, OHLCV |
| `trend_2022.pkl` | 6.7 MB | close, SMA10/30/50 |
| `indicators_2022.pkl` | 7.8 MB | ADX14, RSI14, ±DI, ATR% |

Raw 641 MB deleted after processing. `backtest/build_2022.py` reproduces it.

### Validation

| Check | Expected | Got |
|---|---|---|
| SPY low date | ~Oct 12 2022 | **2022-10-12** |
| Mean RSI (bear year) | below 50 | 48.9 |
| ADX range | ~5–75 | 6.6–73.4 |

The SPY low landing exactly on the true 2022 bottom is the strongest available
confirmation that prices and session handling are right.

Note: the trend series starts 2022-03-16 because SMA50 needs 50 prior bars.
Indicator coverage is March–December, not January–December.

## First 2022 finding: the filters were defensive

How often the bull-put filters would have permitted entry, across the
365-symbol universe:

| Month | Symbol-days | Trend OK | % | + ADX ≥ 20 | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar | 1,980 | 835 | 42% | 456 | 23% |
| Apr | 3,300 | 1,513 | 46% | 917 | 28% |
| **May** | 3,465 | 477 | 14% | 207 | **6%** |
| **Jun** | 3,465 | 443 | 13% | 201 | **6%** |
| Jul | 3,304 | 1,141 | 35% | 298 | 9% |
| Aug | 3,818 | 2,774 | 73% | 1,835 | 48% |
| **Sep** | 3,486 | 875 | 25% | 428 | **12%** |
| **Oct** | 3,486 | 657 | 19% | 347 | 10% |
| Nov | 3,486 | 2,387 | 68% | 1,688 | 48% |
| Dec | 3,486 | 2,139 | 61% | 1,225 | 35% |
| **Total** | 33,276 | 13,241 | 40% | **7,602** | **23%** |

**Only 23% of symbol-days qualified in 2022**, against a far higher rate in
2023. In a year SPY fell ~19%, the filters kept the system mostly flat — which
is the correct behaviour for a strategy that is structurally long.

June (SPY −7.9%) and September (−9.9%) both dropped to 6% and 12% eligibility.
The system would have been nearly idle through the worst months.

### Where it was wrong

Cross-checking eligibility against SPY's monthly move is not uniformly
flattering:

| Month | SPY | Eligible | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mar | +3.7% | 23% | too cautious |
| Apr | −9.0% | 28% | **too permissive — worst miss** |
| May | −0.4% | 6% | fine |
| Jun | −7.9% | 6% | correct |
| Jul | +8.1% | 9% | missed a rally |
| Aug | −3.8% | 48% | **too permissive** |
| Sep | −9.9% | 12% | correct |
| Oct | +5.3% | 10% | missed a rally |
| Nov | +6.0% | 48% | correct |
| Dec | −6.1% | 35% | too permissive |

Trend filters are lagging by construction, so being late at turns is expected —
but **April and August are real misses.** Both were down months where the
filters stayed open at 28% and 48%. August is the worse case: SPY fell 3.8%
while eligibility sat at its joint-highest reading of the year.

This is a lagging-indicator failure mode: August followed a strong July rally,
so SMAs and ADX still read bullish as the market turned over.

**This does not tell us whether 2022 would have been profitable.** Eligibility
is not P&L. A 4% eligibility rate in a falling month can still lose money if
the positions opened were the wrong ones. That question needs option prices.

## Outstanding

Needed from ThetaData for 2022, matching the 2023 schema:

```
trade_date, symbol, expiration, strike, right, bid, ask, delta
```

- **bid and ask**, not trade prices
- **delta** (or enough to compute it from quotes)
- EOD snapshot is sufficient
- Universe: at minimum the 365 symbols in `sectors.json`

When it arrives, the 2023 configuration runs **unchanged** — no re-tuning.
That is the entire point of an out-of-sample test.
