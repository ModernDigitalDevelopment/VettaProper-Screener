Screener Research

How this strategy was derived

Everything on this page comes from backtests against 6.33 million ThetaData end-of-day option records covering 2023. The starting point was a forensic review of 138 real trades that lost $28,529, plus a line-by-line read of four predecessor repositories.

Diagnosis of the $28,529 loss

The first question asked was whether black swan events caused it. They did not. No position suffered a gap attributable to an unforeseeable event. The losses were structural:

84%
Sector concentration

A single sector produced the large majority of the loss. There was no per-sector cap.

35/75
Day-one stop-outs

Round-trip friction ($0.20) against a typical credit ($0.33) made the close quote read 2–3× the credit instantly. Nearly half of all stops fired on entry day.

0
Real orders placed

The automated path ran a simulator with synthetic fills. broker_execution_enabled was hardcoded False. It appeared to work while doing nothing.

The ranking model: delta × risk-reward

To rank candidates honestly, 2,129 spreads were taken without any ranking, then regressed against return on risk (net P&L ÷ max loss). Using return on risk rather than dollar P&L removes the position-sizing confound.

Fitted coefficients

risk:reward+1.1350
risk:reward²+0.2034
delta−1.2623
delta²+2.3618
delta × risk:reward−2.5379

R² = 0.063. That is low, and it should be — single-trade outcomes in a ~77% win-rate strategy are dominated by whether the underlying stayed above a strike. What the fit establishes is the shape: risk-reward drives returns, delta is a penalty, and the negative interaction means a fat credit is worth much less when you had to go close to the money to get it.

Effect of turning ranking on

5% sizing, 2 per sector, all else identical.

+21.5% net, +3.1pp win rate, identical peak capital at risk.

Delta × risk-reward surface

Mean return on risk per trade, from the 2,129-trade unranked sample. Green cells are where the money is.

Read it along a row: at every delta, moving right (higher risk-reward) improves returns. Read it down a column: at every risk-reward, higher delta generally hurts.

Tested configurations

Every row is a full backtest. Sort by whichever column matters to you — but read the drawdown and peak-risk columns before the net column.

Rows with peak risk above 100% are not executable. They had more capital committed than the account contained, which means a margin call, not a return. They are shown for completeness, not as options.

Monthly P&L — recommended configuration

5% per position, 1 per sector, R:R ≥ 0.20, fitted ranking.

Profit concentration: 86% of all profit came from three months (June, November, December 2023). The other four months were roughly flat. A strategy whose returns depend on a few months is far more fragile than its average expectancy suggests.

What these numbers do not prove

  • Seven months, not a year. The option databases cover March, April–June and October–December 2023. Any annual figure is an extrapolation across a gap, not a measured year.
  • One regime. 2023 was a recovery year with falling volatility — close to best case for short-premium strategies. There is no 2022, no COVID crash, no 2018 volatility spike in this sample.
  • End-of-day data. Entries and exits are modelled on EOD quotes. Intraday adverse moves between decision and fill are invisible.
  • Fills are modelled, not observed. 95% of fills assumed at midpoint, 5% crossing the spread. Real fills on multi-leg spreads are worse when markets move.
  • A bug was found and fixed mid-research. An iron condor sizing error produced a −$73 quadrillion artifact by treating near-zero max loss as tradable. Guards were added and affected results recomputed. This is disclosed rather than hidden because it is exactly the class of error that makes backtests lie.
  • Survivorship. The universe is a current index snapshot, so symbols delisted during 2023 are absent.
